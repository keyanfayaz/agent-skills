# Six areas to look at

Every assessment looks at the same six areas, so progress can be compared over time and across people. Each gets one level: Starting, Building or Strong. Levels describe what you can observe about their setup and habits — never a judgment of the person. Give each level one line of evidence ("you've re-explained your role in 7 of the last 20 sessions"). When evidence is thin, say so and pick the lower level.

Recommendations are your own judgment; this list only fixes what you look at.

## Context

Does the agent know who they are and how they work?

- **Starting** — No personal instructions, or only a line or two. They re-explain their role, audience, tone or preferences at the start of many sessions.
- **Building** — Personal instructions exist and cover some of who they are, but key things are missing (their audience, style, tools, boundaries) or out of date. Some re-explaining still happens.
- **Strong** — Instructions cover who they are, what they work on, how they like results, and what to avoid; they're short and current. Re-explaining is rare.
- **Evidence** — Personal instructions files and their age; any memory the tool keeps; how often typed messages start with background the agent should already know.
- **Ask** — "Do you find yourself explaining the same background at the start of chats?" · "Have you ever told Claude/Codex about yourself in its settings?"

## Reuse

Is repeated work captured so it doesn't have to be explained each time?

- **Starting** — The same kinds of requests, checklists or long instructions are typed or pasted again and again. No personal skills or saved templates.
- **Building** — A few skills, templates or saved prompts exist, but the most frequent repeated tasks aren't covered, or existing ones aren't used.
- **Strong** — Their most common repeated tasks are one short command or a skill that triggers on its own, and they use them.
- **Evidence** — Repeated phrasing or pasted blocks across sessions; installed personal skills and whether they appear in history; checklists or templates in their files.
- **Ask** — "What do you ask AI to do every week?" · "Is there a set of instructions you keep copying in?"

## Connections

Are their tools and information connected, or copied in by hand?

- **Starting** — Information from email, calendar, documents or other apps is pasted in by hand. Nothing is connected.
- **Building** — One or two tools are connected, but the places they spend most time (or most often paste from) aren't.
- **Strong** — The tools they rely on most are connected, and the agent reads from them directly when it needs to.
- **Evidence** — Connected tools and MCP servers; long pasted emails, documents or data in history; requests like "here's what my calendar says".
- **Ask** — "Which apps do you copy things out of to give to AI?" · "Which tools would you most like it to see directly?"

## Delegation

Do they hand off whole tasks, or only ask quick questions?

- **Starting** — Mostly one-off questions and rewrites. Multi-step work is done by hand, with AI used for small pieces.
- **Building** — Some multi-step tasks are handed off, but with step-by-step steering, or they're abandoned partway.
- **Strong** — They regularly hand off whole tasks with a clear goal and check the result, and they know when to plan first.
- **Evidence** — Session length and shape; how often they steer step by step; abandoned sessions; use of planning, background tasks or helpers where the tool offers them.
- **Ask** — "What's the biggest task you've handed to AI from start to finish?" · "Where do you stop and take over yourself?"

## Safety

Are permissions, secrets and checking handled sensibly?

- **Starting** — Secrets (API keys, passwords) appear in chats or settings in plain text; or approvals are switched off everywhere; or output is used without checking.
- **Building** — No obvious secret leaks, but permissions are either very loose or so tight they constantly interrupt; checking is ad hoc.
- **Strong** — No secrets in chats or settings; permissions allow routine work without blanket "skip all approvals"; they review anything that goes to other people or touches important files.
- **Evidence** — Settings and permission rules; key-like strings in history or settings (report that one exists and where — never the value); how often they accept or deny approvals.
- **Ask** — "Have you ever pasted a password or key into a chat?" · "Do you check AI's work before it goes to someone else?"

## Habits

How much, and how widely, do they use AI?

- **Starting** — Occasional use, for one or two kinds of task.
- **Building** — Regular use for several kinds of task, but clear parts of their work never involve AI.
- **Strong** — Daily use across most of their work, and they try it on new kinds of task when they come up.
- **Evidence** — Sessions per week; the range of topics in typed messages; which tools they use; gaps between their role's work and what they use AI for.
- **Ask** — "How often do you use AI in a normal week?" · "What part of your work have you never tried it on?"
